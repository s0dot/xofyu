# Roblox IP fan games: case studies and enforcement patterns

*Research note for **Algebraic!** (GDD §5). Compiled 2026-10-12. This is not legal advice.*

**Tag key:** **[Docs]** means I read Roblox's official creator-docs snapshot (2026-10-10) directly. **[Web]** means a web search result, with its URL. WebFetch was DNS-blocked for every domain I tried (wikipedia, pockettactics, rowatcher), so every [Web] claim rests on **search-engine snippets**, not full articles. **[Memory]** means my prior knowledge, which I did not re-verify. **[Inference]** means my own analysis.
**Budget used:** 28 web searches. I did not clone any repos because none were relevant.

---

## 0. The short answer: how do JJS, Blox Fruits and the other anime games get away with it?

1. **They don't have a legal exemption. They are tolerated, and they can be removed at any time.** Roblox's own docs tell creators: *"If you want to use an IP in your game, you must have explicit permission from the rights holder. We recommend removing any content that might use IP and isn't covered by a license."* They also warn that unlicensed IP use *"might result in the removal of the content and your Roblox account."* [Docs: `ip-licensing/faq.md`, `ip-licensing/creators.md`]
2. **Enforcement depends on complaints, and the rights holder decides whether to complain.** Roblox removes games when a rights holder files a DMCA notice or a Rights Manager request. It does not police anime fan games on its own initiative. [Docs: `production/publishing/ip-guidelines.md`, `rights-manager.md`] The anime giants are owned by Japanese manga publishers and production committees (Shueisha, Toei, MAPPA committees and others). Over 2019–2026 I found almost no record of those owners enforcing on Roblox. The one clear exception (Shinobi Life 2) came through **Viz Media**, Shueisha's US arm. [Web, see table]
3. **Renaming does not create legal protection. It is a way to stay less visible and look less official.** Swapping the IP name for codenames like "The Strongest Hero" or "Vessel" lowers trademark exposure, keeps the game out of searches for the brand, and avoids Roblox's text filter. The Roblox wiki says Roblox filtered "Pokémon" and "Mario" out of game names after the 2018 removals [Web: https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Copyright_and_trademark_infringement]. Copying a character's design is still copyright infringement, whatever the character is called. [Inference]
4. **The rename is usually done before any claim arrives, not in response to one.** Blox Piece became Blox Fruits through a self-described "Anti-Copyright Update" (Update 9, 25 Dec 2019) with no documented claim behind it [Web: https://rblxguide.com/games/blox-fruits/updates/update-9]. Saitama Battlegrounds became The Strongest Battlegrounds, and I found no public reason for that rename [Web: https://sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/5-things-know-playing-roblox-the-strongest-battlegrounds].
5. **The games that do get removed share risk factors.** The owner is a Western or Western-arm rights holder with an active brand-policing culture (Nintendo, Paramount/Nickelodeon, Toho, SEGA, Viz). The owner, or its licensed partner, has its own Roblox product. The game uses ripped official assets or likenesses, carries the IP name in its title, and earns a lot. **Adventure Time scores high on almost all of these** (§4).
6. **From 2025 the outcome can be a bill rather than a takedown.** License Manager lets a registered rights holder scan Roblox, find your game and send a license offer. That offer **activates on its own after 14 days unless you dispute it**, and it takes a revenue share of 0–95%. [Docs: `ip-licensing/license-manager.md`] This gives a large unlicensed game a way to become legitimate. None of the big Shueisha-IP games can use it unless Shueisha or Toei register, and I found no evidence that they have.

## 2. Base rate: how common is enforcement?

- **Numerator (documented cases).** Across 2018–2026 my searches surfaced about **10–12 publicly reported IP removal events** for named Roblox games: Brick Bronze; the Mario batch; Sonic Eclipse; Spongy ×3; Survivor; Shinobi Life 2; Kaiju Universe ×2; the Anime Adventures/AFS/AFS X cluster; and Vox Seas (later restored). Small games' removals are rarely reported, and Roblox does not publish a per-game DMCA log that I could find. **The true count is therefore much higher, but those removals hit tiny games.** [Web, Inference]
- **Denominator.** "Anime-inspired" is a whole Roblox category, with thousands of games [Memory]. **None of the top-tier anime fan games has a documented rights-holder takedown:** Blox Fruits (about 60B visits), TSB (about 19B), JJS (4–7B), GPO, YBA, AUT, Demonfall/Project Slayers, Heroes Battlegrounds, Blue Lock Rivals. [Web, Inference]
- **Estimate [Inference, low confidence].** For a typical anime-IP fan game, the chance of a rights-holder takedown in a given year is **well under 1%**. Two kinds of game face a much higher risk:
  - games using **Western-owned or Western-arm IP whose owner has a Roblox enforcement history** (Nintendo, Paramount, Toho, SEGA, Viz);
  - games that **sit next to a licensed partner's product** (Gamefam).

  Two of the four case clusters with a known trigger followed a licensed or official Roblox product (Spongy/Nickverse and AA/AFS with Gamefam MHA), and Sonic Eclipse came after SEGA's licensed *Sonic Speed Simulator*.
- **Bias warning [Inference].** This sample is survivorship-biased and comes mostly from fan wikis and gaming press. The "<1%" figure means "rare for anime." It does not mean "rare for any IP."

## 4. Roblox's own role

- **DMCA channel.** Notices go to `copyright_agent@roblox.com` or through the IP Platform. Creators can counter-notify, and Roblox *"generally restore[s] the content within 14 business days"* unless the reporter shows it has filed a lawsuit. Repeat infringers may lose their accounts. [Docs: `production/publishing/ip-guidelines.md`]
- **Rights Manager (2024).** The DevForum post "Introducing Rights Manager" is dated **17 Apr 2024** and says rollout reached **100% on 26 Mar 2024** [Web: https://devforum.roblox.com/t/introducing-rights-manager/2880717]. It is open to **any verified rights holder, *including those without a Roblox presence*.** One request can include **up to 250 links**, and each has a status of Pending, Approved (removed) or Rejected. [Docs: `production/publishing/rights-manager.md`] [Inference] This made removals much cheaper for rights holders. One third-party blog claims enforcement speed rose afterward, "particularly from anime IP holders," but I could not verify that [Web: https://rowatcher.com/news/roblox-content-moderation-a-developer-s-survival-guide].
- **License Manager (Jul 2025; self-serve Nov 2025).** The launch partners were Lionsgate, Netflix, SEGA and Kodansha (Blue Lock, TenSura) [Docs: `ip-licensing/index.md`; Web: https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/07/roblox-launches-new-licensing-platform-for-experiences]. It opened to all IP owners in Nov 2025 [Web: https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/roblox-now-allows-self-handling-of-licenses-for-all-ip-owners]. **How "reactive licensing" works** [Docs: `ip-licensing/license-manager.md`, `get-started.md`, `faq.md`]:
  - The rights holder builds an **IP family** of keywords plus at least **10 approved images**. Roblox scans **daily** and lists **"Matches"**: public, actively played games "that use a significant amount of content related to your IP." The detail page for each match includes **gameplay screenshots captured by Roblox**. The rights holder can also manually request a match for up to 3 games a day. **Matches cover perpetual licenses only.**
  - **Roblox does not send the offers. The rights holder does.** A standard offer **auto-activates after 14 days** unless the creator disputes it. A conditional offer asks for changes within 14 days and does not auto-activate. The revenue share is **0–95%**. Rights holders can attempt to license a game twice; *"if an agreement can't be reached … they can pursue other options, such as filing a DMCA claim."*
  - The docs also note: *"If early IP usage is detected in a creator's game, the license request or accepted license agreement may be activated early."*
  - Official framing: *"IP licensing provides rights holders and creators with an alternative to DMCA takedowns."* [Docs: `faq.md`]
  - Reported terms: SEGA's *Like a Dragon* license is reported at a 50% split with a 1,000-DAU minimum. Netflix's is reported at about 15%, though the snippet was unclear about which party gets it. A typical range of 10–25% is reported [Web: https://kidscreen.com/?p=224295, https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/roblox-now-allows-self-handling-of-licenses-for-all-ip-owners].
- **Has Roblox offered reactive licenses to existing anime fan games?**
  - **Roblox itself does not send them.** The offers come from rights holders.
  - **The only concrete hint I found is *Blue Lock: Rivals*.** Its listing reportedly says it is "allowed under the Roblox License Manager but not officially supervised or approved by Kodansha." That wording fits a matched-offer agreement, but I could not confirm it a second time. **[Low–Med]**
  - **One former unlicensed anime developer (Gomu, Anime Adventures) moved to a proactive license** (*TenSura: Isekai Adventures*, Kodansha IP) [Web: allthings.how above].
  - **I found no evidence that Shueisha, Toei or Shogakukan have joined License Manager.** As far as I could find, the JJK, One Piece, OPM, Naruto, Demon Slayer, JoJo and MHA fan games cannot be reactively licensed today. [Web: no results; Inference]
